# Professional Single-Stock Pitch Deck: Structure, Content and Visual Design (16:9 PowerPoint)

Scope note: about 23 searches and fetches (Nov 2026). The primary sources reached were training-site guides (Mergers & Inquisitions, Wall Street Prep, Wall Street Playbook, Deckary), Pershing Square disclaimer documents, CFA Research Challenge material, and two machine-readable "bank style" design specs: Anthropic's financial-services pitch-deck skill and the SlideSpeak pitch-book prompt. No full Sohn or activist deck could be read slide by slide. They are mostly paywalled or sit behind large PDFs, so the conference-deck evidence is thin and is flagged below. Items marked "estimate" or "inference" are not sourced facts.

## 1. Structure: which slides, in what order, and how many (short 5–8 vs full 15–25)

### Takeaway
The guides agree on a fixed spine: Recommendation (long/short, price, target, % upside), then Business overview, then a Thesis of 2–3 points (the "variant perception"), then Catalysts (6–12 months), then Valuation with Base/Upside/Downside cases, then Risks and mitigants, with an optional summary slide at the end. Short interview or competition pitches run about 5–10 minutes and fit 5–8 slides. Bank pitch books run 15–25 slides. Activist and conference decks can run much longer, but that is unverified here.

### Cited Findings
- M&I recommends six sections in this order for all stock pitches: (1) Recommendation: long or short, current share price, how far the stock is mispriced, and what it should be worth; no "neutral" calls unless the company was assigned to you. (2) Company Background: products, revenue, EBITDA, market cap, current multiples; a price/volume chart earns "bonus points". (3) Investment Thesis: **2–3** factors the market has missed. (4) Catalysts: events in the next **6–12 months**. (5) Valuation: DCF as the primary method. (6) Risk Factors and Mitigants: **2–3** company-specific risks — [Mergers & Inquisitions, Stock Pitch Guide](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/stock-pitch-guide/)
- M&I ties each thesis factor to a specific per-share impact, "to show the mispricing is more than theoretical" — [Mergers & Inquisitions](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/stock-pitch-guide/)
- M&I's Recommendation section packs the whole pitch up front: long/short, current price, % mispricing, the top 2–3 reasons the price should change in 6–12 months, 2–3 catalysts, and 2–3 risks with hedges. A summary slide at the end is offered as an alternative visual layout — [Mergers & Inquisitions, Bank Stock Pitch](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/bank-stock-pitch)
- In M&I's PowerPoint version the valuation section runs as a football field, then a slide summarising long-term projections, then sensitivities. The price/volume chart appears in the PowerPoint version but not the Word version — [Mergers & Inquisitions, Bank Stock Pitch](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/bank-stock-pitch)
- On length, M&I says an initial pitch should be "2-3 pages at the most", expanded only if you have more time, are asked to build slides, or are given a page count. Take-home pitches typically get a few days to a week; time-pressured case studies get 2–4 hours — [Mergers & Inquisitions](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/stock-pitch-guide/)
- A hedge-fund interview guide uses six parts with timings: **Thesis (30 s)**, a one-sentence long/short with company, price and expected upside/downside; **Business Overview (60 s)**; **Variant Perception (90 s)**, defined as "What you see that the market is missing, this is your edge"; **Catalysts (60 s)**; **Valuation (60 s)**, comps and/or DCF to the price target; **Risks (30 s)**. That totals about 5.5 minutes. It advises addressing the bear case up front — [Wall Street Playbook, Hedge Fund Interview Prep](https://wallstreetplaybook.org/hedge-fund-interview-prep)
- A paid guide sells 2-, 3- and 8-minute example pitches and an "8-part stock pitch framework (Recommendation → Catalysts → Valuation → Risks)". It calls variant perception and risk/reward central to a Point72-style approach. This is a vendor claim and was not verified — [StreetReady Prep (Gumroad)](https://streetready.gumroad.com/l/StockPitchGuide)
- Another guide gives the classic order as stock recommendation, company overview, investment thesis, catalyst, valuation and returns, risks, mitigating factors. This came from a search snippet and the attribution is not fully verified — [FE Training, How to Pitch a Stock](https://www.fe.training/?p=10020888)
- Career Principles frames the pitch as evidence that tells a narrative toward a buy or short recommendation, built on "3-4 drivers" of the expected price change. This came from a search snippet — [Career Principles, Stock Pitch](https://careerprinciples.com/resources/stock-pitch)
- Bank pitch-book lengths: initial pitch meetings 15–20 slides, typical range 15–25; sell-side M&A books 30–50; IPO books 40–60. Per section: Executive Summary 1–2 slides, Situation Overview 2–3, Market Analysis 3–5, Company Analysis 3–5, Valuation 5–15, Recommendations 3–8, Next Steps 1–2, plus an Appendix — [Deckary, Investment Banking Pitch Book](https://deckary.com/blog/investment-banking-pitch-book)
- The bank pitch-book cover carries bank branding, client name, transaction type, date and a confidentiality notice — [Deckary](https://deckary.com/blog/investment-banking-pitch-book)
- CFA Institute Research Challenge teams present for **10 minutes**, then 10 minutes of Q&A — [CFA Institute Research Challenge, Volunteers](https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/researchchallenge/volunteers); [Knowt notes](https://knowt.com/note/a12ae282-df44-437f-9f0b-0c7a3dd619a1/p1)

### Inferences
- **Short pitch (6–8 slides), estimate based on the cited structures:**
  1. Title/Recommendation: ticker, rating, price, 12-month target, % upside, bear/base/bull range, and 3 one-line thesis points.
  2. Business overview and key metrics, with a price/volume chart.
  3. Investment thesis or variant view: 3 numbered points, each with a quantified per-share or per-metric impact.
  4. Catalysts / what to watch: a dated timeline over 6–12 months, with hard catalysts flagged.
  5. Valuation: a football field of methods with current price and target lines, plus a multiples table.
  6. Scenarios: a bear/base/bull table with drivers and implied prices, plus a risk/reward bar.
  7. Risks and mitigants: 2–4 company-specific risks, each with a mitigant or "signpost".
  8. Disclaimer and sources.
- **Full pitch (15–25 slides), estimate:** add an agenda or tracker; a 1–2 slide executive summary; industry and competitive position (2–3); financial history and forecast vs consensus (2–3); one slide per thesis pillar (3); peer comparison or factor scorecard (1–2); DCF, multiples and sensitivity (2–4); scenario bridge (1); management and capital allocation (1); and an appendix with model detail and a full source list.
- Mapping the stored research-report fields to slides:
  - **rating** → title slide badge.
  - **12-month bear/base/bull** → title slide range bar and the scenario slide.
  - **valuation methods** → football field.
  - **factor score vs peers** → a peer scorecard (heat-map table) on the business or valuation slide.
  - **key metrics** → a stat strip on the overview slide.
  - **risks** → the risks/mitigants table.
  - **what to watch** → the catalyst timeline.
  - **cited sources** → per-slide source lines plus an appendix source list.
- Because the guides put the whole conclusion on slide 1, the title slide should carry the rating, target and upside, not just a company name. That is the main difference from a corporate deck cover.

### Gaps
- No verified slide-by-slide count for actual Sohn or Robin Hood conference decks. Activist decks are often described as very long; my unverified recollection is that Pershing Square's Dec-2012 Herbalife short presentation ran about 340 slides. Treat that as unconfirmed.
- No source gave a words-per-slide count specific to stock pitches. See question 3 for bullet limits.

For a deck generated from a stored research report and read on screen or in print, the bank and sell-side idiom fits better.
- Morgan Stanley's bear/base/bull risk-reward presentation is the clearest sell-side precedent for a "12-month price range bear/base/bull" visual.

## 3. Bank pitch-book design conventions: action titles, trackers, footnotes, page numbers, disclaimers, charts, text density

### Takeaway
Bank-style slides use one message per slide, often as a full-sentence "action title". Title position, logo, page number and footer sit in identical places on every slide. Every number carries a source line and footnote in small italic at the bottom. Exhibits dominate text, with roughly 6–7 bullets per box and 2 lines per bullet at most. Charts must still read in black and white.

### Cited Findings
- Title position, logo placement, page numbers and footer information should be in identical locations on every slide; "inconsistency signals carelessness". Layouts should be identical throughout, and dense analysis split across 2–3 slides rather than crammed — [Deckary](https://deckary.com/blog/investment-banking-pitch-book)
- Charts, graphs and tables should dominate over text, with no explanatory paragraphs. Any chart needing several paragraphs of explanation should be redesigned — [Deckary](https://deckary.com/blog/investment-banking-pitch-book)
- Charts must be readable when printed in black and white — [Deckary](https://deckary.com/blog/investment-banking-pitch-book)
- Transaction comps: 5–15 deals, sorted by date (newest first) or by multiple (high to low). Show mean, median, 25th and 75th percentile for key multiples — [Deckary](https://deckary.com/blog/investment-banking-pitch-book)
- Anthropic's financial-services pitch-deck skill (bank-template conventions):
  - Bullets: at most 6–7 per content box and 2 lines each. Use ✓ for included or positive items, × for excluded or negative, • for neutral lists, 1. 2. 3. for sequences and – for sub-bullets. Avoid orphan words.
  - Same-level boxes use identical font sizes. Stacked boxes share left margin, bullet indent and width; side-by-side boxes share top position and height.
  - Logo top-right at a consistent size.
  - Tables must be real table objects. Text columns are left-aligned and numeric columns right- or centre-aligned. The header row is bold with brand-colour shading and white text. A total or mean row is bold, with a separator line above and distinct shading.
  - Charts fill their area, never thumbnails.

  — [Anthropic financial-services pitch-deck skill (ClaudePluginHub mirror)](https://www.claudepluginhub.com/skills/anthropics-investment-banking-plugins-vertical-plugins-investment-banking/pitch-deck)
- The same skill's number formatting: multiples to 1 decimal place (9.69 → 9.7x); CAGR to a whole percent or the nearest 0.5%; market sizes of $10bn or more to the nearest $1bn; rounding "should not materially change the figure" — [Anthropic pitch-deck skill](https://www.claudepluginhub.com/skills/anthropics-investment-banking-plugins-vertical-plugins-investment-banking/pitch-deck)
- A vendor "pitch book" design spec (illustrative, not a bank standard) describes:
  - Full-sentence action titles, e.g. "The methodologies converge on a range of $850 to $1,050mm" or "We recommend a targeted process at an indicative value of $975mm".
  - An uppercase letter-spaced "kicker" above the title, i.e. a section tracker.
  - A full-width 1px navy rule under the title, with a short gold accent segment flush left.
  - A footer with a 1px top rule, wordmark, 7px disclaimer and page number in a small navy square.
  - A "STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL" mark top-right.
  - An agenda in Roman numerals (I–IV plus Appendix A) with dot leaders to page numbers.
  - 0px corner radius, no shadows or gradients.
  - Deliberately dense layout, warning against sparse minimalist slides with oversized display type.

  — [SlideSpeak, Pitch Book design prompt](https://slidespeak.co/slide-design-prompts/prompts/pitch-book)
- In the same spec, comps tables have a navy header row, zebra rows (#ffffff / #f4f6f8), right-aligned tabular figures, negatives in parentheses, units in the column head, and bold Mean/Median rows above a 2px accent rule. The recommendation slide uses numbered points with bold lead-ins, a key-terms callout box and a chevron timeline — [SlideSpeak](https://slidespeak.co/slide-design-prompts/prompts/pitch-book)
- Macabacus "Deck Check" flags inconsistencies in formatting, layout, content and grammar, and replaces stray formatting across a deck. Its team colour palettes include modelling colours (blue for inputs, green for links) — [Macabacus Deck Check](https://help.macabacus.com/article/282-deck-check); [Macabacus Color Palettes](https://help.macabacus.com/article/766-color-palettes)
- M&I advises summarising valuation output rather than pasting raw sensitivity tables or copying filing language, and using plain language — [Mergers & Inquisitions](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/stock-pitch-guide/)

### Inferences
- Template rule set (inference from the above):
  - Each content slide has an action title (1 sentence, at most 2 lines, about 10–20 words; estimate) stating the takeaway with a number in it, e.g. "Base case implies $142 (+23%) on 18x FY27 EPS".
  - An optional kicker or tracker above the title names the section, e.g. "VALUATION".
  - Each slide has one dominant exhibit, plus a right-hand or bottom "key takeaways" box of 3–5 bullets.
  - A source line and notes sit bottom-left, and the page number bottom-right, on every slide except the cover.
- Total words per content slide: estimate about 60–120 words of text excluding table cells. This is derived from 6–7 bullets of 2 lines or fewer, plus a title; no source gives a count.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source documents the "tracker" convention: a row of section names or numbered tabs across the top with the current section highlighted. It is widespread in bank books, but here it is only seen indirectly through the vendor's "kicker".
- No bank's internal style guide was obtainable.

## 4. Typography: fonts, title and body sizes, minimum and footnote sizes

### Takeaway
Concrete sourced sizes for bank-style PowerPoint: body 11–14pt, table body 9–11pt, table header 10–12pt bold, footnotes and sources 8–9pt italic. Title sizes conflict across sources (40–48pt vs about 20–23pt-equivalent). Full-sentence action titles in practice need the smaller range. Named fonts in bank decks are not sourced. The vendor spec pairs a serif title font (EB Garamond) with a sans body font (Arimo, metric-compatible with Arial).

  | Body / bullets | 11–14pt |
  | Table header | 10–12pt bold |
  | Table body | 9–11pt |

### Inferences
- Unit conversion (inference): a standard 16:9 PowerPoint slide is 13.333 × 7.5 in (960 × 540 pt). If the vendor spec assumes a 1280px-wide canvas, 1px ≈ 0.75pt. That makes its action title about 20–23pt, body about 8–10pt and footnotes about 6pt. The canvas width is not stated, so this is an assumption.
- Recommended template sizes (estimate, reconciling the sources for a dense, readable 16:9 deck):
  - Action title: 20–24pt bold, 1–2 lines.
  - Kicker or tracker: 9–10pt caps.
  - Subtitle: 12–14pt.
  - Body: 11–12pt (10pt minimum).
  - Table body: 9–10pt.
  - Chart labels: 9pt.
  - Source lines: 8pt italic.
  - Disclaimer page body: 8–9pt.
  - Title-slide headline: 32–44pt (this is where the 40–48pt figure fits).
- Font choice (estimate, unsourced): use system-safe fonts so the .pptx renders identically on any machine: Arial or Calibri for body, optionally Georgia or Times New Roman for titles. At most two families. Arimo/Liberation Sans are metric-compatible fallbacks for Arial when rendering in LibreOffice.

### Gaps
- No primary source names the fonts actually used by specific banks or funds (e.g., Arial vs Times New Roman vs a brand font). No sourced minimum readable size beyond the 8–9pt footnote convention.

## 5. Colour: palettes, scenario and rating colour coding, light vs dark, printing

### Takeaway
The sourced convention is a white background, dark text (#000000 or #333333), one brand colour (typically navy) for headers, table header rows and rules, and one accent. Charts must survive black-and-white printing. No source covered bull/base/bear or buy/hold/sell colour coding; the convention proposed below is an inference.

### Cited Findings
- Body text should be #000000 or #333333 on white or light backgrounds, with white text on dark fills. Headers and accents use the template's brand colour. Table header rows use brand-colour shading with white text. Bright yellow or orange boxes are placeholders to remove, not design elements — [Anthropic pitch-deck skill](https://www.claudepluginhub.com/skills/anthropics-investment-banking-plugins-vertical-plugins-investment-banking/pitch-deck)

  - Buy/Overweight = green, Hold/Neutral = grey or amber, Sell/Underweight = red.
- Because of the black-and-white rule, always label the scenario name and value in text on or beside the bar, and do not rely on hue alone. Use different lightness values, e.g. dark navy base vs mid-tone bull and bear.

## 6. Valuation visuals: football field, scenario tables, price-target bridges, sensitivity tables

### Takeaway
The football field is the core summary exhibit. It is a horizontal floating-bar chart with one bar per method: DCF, trading comps, precedents, 52-week range, and optionally analyst targets, LBO or SOTP. A vertical line marks the current price, and another the target or offer, with dollar labels at bar ends. Pitch decks add Base/Upside/Downside implied-price tables showing asymmetry, a long-term projections slide and a compact sensitivity grid. Waterfall charts are the standard "bridge" form.

### Cited Findings
- WSP calls the football field "a floating bar chart in Excel that puts several valuation analyses side-by-side". Its standard rows are DCF, LBO, comparable companies, precedent transactions and the 52-week high/low, with liquidation and sum-of-the-parts optional. The goal is to "sanity-check various methodologies against one another". It appears in pitch books and fairness opinions — [Wall Street Prep, Football Field Valuation](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/football-field-valuation-real-example-excel-template/)
- The 52-week trading range and analyst price targets are reference points, not valuation methods. Precedents typically sit above trading comps because of control premiums. Takeover-style rows (transactions, % premium paid, DCF with synergies) are often shown separately from standalone rows. Input ranges should be narrow enough that bars are not too wide or too narrow. These points came from search snippets — [FE Training, Football Field Analysis](https://www.fe.training/free-resources/valuation/football-field-analysis/); [Ryan O'Connell Finance](https://ryanoconnellfinance.com/football-field-valuation/)
- The vendor spec's football field has horizontal floating bars (#5c7ea3, 1px navy border), dollar labels at bar ends, a dashed 2px gold offer-price line crossing all rows with a label chip, and 1px light gridlines. Its example rows: 52-week $720–890mm, comps $850–1,050mm, precedents $900–1,100mm, DCF $880–1,080mm, LBO $780–950mm on a $700–1,200mm axis, with an offer line at $975mm — [SlideSpeak](https://slidespeak.co/slide-design-prompts/prompts/pitch-book)
- Pitch valuation should show Base, Upside and Downside cases linked to revenue and expense drivers, with the implied share price for each. Asymmetry targets for a long: Base about 20–30% undervalued, Upside 50%+, Downside only about 5–10% overvalued; shorts are the mirror image. M&I advises against stating explicit probabilities, since you will be asked how you derived them — [Mergers & Inquisitions](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/stock-pitch-guide/)
- Multiples can support the DCF but should not carry the argument alone. Summarise sensitivities rather than pasting full tables — [Mergers & Inquisitions](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/stock-pitch-guide/)
- Standard pitch-book visuals include football fields, trading comps tables, transaction timelines and **waterfall charts for valuation bridges**; Mekko charts for segmentation — [Deckary](https://deckary.com/blog/investment-banking-pitch-book)
- Morgan Stanley's research framework presents bear, base and bull cases to show risk around the expected outcome — [EconBiz record (snippet)](https://econbiz.de/Record/the-risk-reward-framework-at-morgan-stanley-research-srinivasan-suraj/10013109175)

### Inferences
- Exhibits for the automated template (inference):
  1. **Football field**: one row per valuation method from the report, a vertical solid line for the current price and a dashed accent line for the base-case target, with low and high labels at bar ends. Put the 52-week range in a visually distinct (lighter) row because it is a reference, not a method.
  2. **12-month range bar**: a single horizontal bar from bear to bull, with markers at bear, base, bull and current price. Label each with price and % vs current. This is a compact version for the title slide.
  3. **Scenario table**: columns Bear / Base / Bull; rows for key driver assumptions (e.g., revenue growth, margin, exit multiple), implied price and % upside/downside.
  4. **Price-target bridge**: a waterfall from the current price to the base target, showing contributions from earnings growth, multiple change and capital return.
  5. **Sensitivity grid**: 5×5 at most, e.g. target multiple × EPS or WACC × terminal growth, with the base cell highlighted.

### Gaps

## 7. How sources are cited on slides

### Takeaway
Each exhibit gets a bottom-of-slide source line, e.g. "Source: Company filings, FactSet as of June 2026." It is followed by numbered "Notes:" matching superscripts in the body. Use 8–9pt italic in a full-width footnote box. Training sites stress that every figure must be sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Format: a "Sources:" line first, then "Notes:" with numbered entries separated by semicolons. Every superscript number in the slide body needs a matching note. The footnote box is about **32.5 cm wide on 16:9** (about 24 cm on 4:3), in 8–9pt italic — [Anthropic pitch-deck skill](https://www.claudepluginhub.com/skills/anthropics-investment-banking-plugins-vertical-plugins-investment-banking/pitch-deck)
- Every data point, statistic, comparable and precedent needs a source citation in small footer text. Typical sources named: Capital IQ, FactSet, company filings, PitchBook, public disclosures — [Deckary](https://deckary.com/blog/investment-banking-pitch-book)
- Example source footnote from the vendor spec: "Source: Company filings, FactSet as of June 2026." Its source footnotes are set at 8px — [SlideSpeak](https://slidespeak.co/slide-design-prompts/prompts/pitch-book)
- M&I advises keeping notes of key numbers and their sources for Q&A — [Mergers & Inquisitions](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/stock-pitch-guide/)
- Pershing Square's 2026 deck states that its analyses and conclusions "are based on publicly available information" — [PSH 2026 Annual Investor Presentation](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/11144917/2026-Annual-Investor-Presentation.pdf)

### Inferences
- Template: a bottom-left footnote box spanning the content width. A standard 16:9 slide is 33.867 cm wide, so 32.5 cm implies about 0.7 cm side margins. Use the pattern `Source: {provider list}; {doc} ({date}). Notes: (1) …; (2) …`, with "Market data as of {date}" on every slide showing prices.
- Map the report's cited sources to per-slide lines: only the sources used on that slide. Add a final "Sources" appendix slide with the full list, including URLs, filing types and access dates.

## 8. Disclaimer slide wording norms for non-advice research

### Takeaway
Fund and activist decks open or close with a dense legal page. It states the material is for informational purposes only and is not investment advice or a recommendation. It says it is not an offer or solicitation, that analyses rest on publicly available information, and that forward-looking statements carry risks with no obligation to update. Conflicts are disclosed, and the author may hold and change positions.

### Cited Findings
- Pershing Square 2019 deck: "All information provided herein is for informational purposes only and should not be deemed as a recommendation to buy or sell any security mentioned" — [PSH 2019 Annual Investor Update](https://pershingsquareholdings.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2019-Annual-Investor-Update-Presentation.pdf)
- Pershing Square 2022 deck: the material "does not constitute an offer to sell or the solicitation of an offer to purchase any security or investment product" — [PSH 2022 Annual Investor Presentation](https://pershingsquareholdings.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2022-Annual-Investor-Presentation.pdf)
- Pershing Square 2026 deck: "The analyses and conclusions of Pershing Square contained in this presentation are based on publicly available information" — [PSH 2026 Annual Investor Presentation](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/11144917/2026-Annual-Investor-Presentation.pdf)
- PSH website: contents are "for your personal use and informational purposes only and are subject to change without notice", and nothing "constitutes investment, legal, tax or other advice" — [PSH Portfolio page](https://pershingsquareholdings.com/portfolio/)
- A Pershing Square / Robinhood communication (April 2026, PDF text extracted) includes:
  - "distributed for informational purposes only and should not be considered investment advice or a recommendation of any particular security, strategy or investment product".
  - A conflict-of-interest clause: views are the speaker's own, and the affiliate may have a conflict that "could affect the objectivity of this communication".
  - A forward-looking statements clause saying not to "place undue reliance", with no obligation "to update any forward-looking statements for any reason".
  - Offer/solicitation language.

  — [Pershing Square, Robinhood Disclaimer (PDF)](https://pershingsquare.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/Robinhood+Disclaimer.pdf)
- CFA Research Challenge reports must use CFA Institute's prescribed cover and back pages (where disclosures sit) — [CFA Institute 2015 RC presentation](https://higherlogicdownload.s3.amazonaws.com/CFAI/325a9907-325e-47be-8b9a-e1ceb0567276/UploadedImages/documents/RC/2015_ReseachChallenge_presentation.pdf)

### Inferences
- Clause checklist for an automated non-advice research deck (composite inferred from the cited wording; not legal advice):
  1. Informational and educational purposes only; not investment, legal or tax advice.
  2. Not a recommendation or an offer or solicitation to buy or sell any security.
  3. Based on publicly available information and third-party data believed reliable but not guaranteed; no representation or warranty as to accuracy.
  4. Opinions as of {date}, subject to change without notice; no obligation to update.
  5. Forward-looking statements, estimates, price targets and scenarios are inherently uncertain; actual results may differ materially.
  6. Author or tool position disclosure, and whether positions may change.
  7. Past performance is not indicative of future results; investing involves risk of loss.
  8. For automated tools: generated by software or a model and may contain errors; verify independently.
- Placement: slide 2 (after the cover, as in activist and fund decks) or the final slide. Use 8–9pt body in one or two columns, plus a short one-line footer version on each slide, e.g. "For informational purposes only. Not investment advice." The vendor spec runs a 7px footer disclaimer on every slide — [SlideSpeak](https://slidespeak.co/slide-design-prompts/prompts/pitch-book)
